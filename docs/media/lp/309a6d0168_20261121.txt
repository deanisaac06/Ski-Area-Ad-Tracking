There’s a joy that comes from the mountains that’s impossible to replicate anywhere else. It’s the way a great run can make everything else fall quiet. It’s the relationships we build with people who share our passion. It’s the power of movement, combined with inspiration from nature that keeps us coming back.
When life gets difficult, those experiences can become something more: a source of strength and solace when we need them most. 
In 2025, My Ikon: My Joy explored the science behind the happiness we experience on snow, from the benefits of movement and fresh air, to the benefits of friendship and connection. 
This year, that science becomes even more relevant. My Ikon: My Journey is a two-part series about two different lifelong skiers, Lucas and Emmy, whose love for the mountains has helped them through life’s hardest moments. Both skiers have faced distinctive challenges, but share a determination to reclaim their sense of self in the mountains they love. 
LUCAS | ARAPAHOE BASIN 
For Lucas Herbert, skiing was a passion long before it became his profession. Ski films fueled his imagination and opened his eyes to a world he dreamed of joining. But when mental health struggles took him to some of life’s darkest places, that passion became his lifeline while he sought the help he needed. 
Years later, Lucas worked to land a job behind the camera at Arapahoe Basin, capturing the sport that inspired him through those difficult years. Surrounded by a community of people who shared his love of skiing, he built a future he once struggled to imagine. 
The mountains are places for people to come together and heal—Lucas
In connection with Lucas’s story, Ikon Pass is making a donation to Building Hope Summit County, a nonprofit of his choice dedicated to supporting mental wellness and expanding access to care in mountain communities.
WATCH LUCAS’S STORY
EMMY | STEAMBOAT
Emmy fell in love with skiing at five years old. It’s a love she’s carried throughout her life, through countless days on snow and friendships built along the way. But when complications from type 1 diabetes led to vision loss, the independence she’d always known was suddenly threatened.
At Steamboat, Emmy experienced a pivotal moment, returning to the sport she loved with friends she knew she could trust. Getting back on skis wasn’t easy. But on the mountain, she began rebuilding her confidence and taking back the freedom that had always meant so much to her. Emmy was able to bring this sense of confidence and joy into other areas of her life, which transformed her relationship to living with vision loss.
The first day I was terrified, but when we started the run, I felt capable—Emmy
In support of Emmy’s journey, Ikon Pass is making a donation to the recipient of Emmy’s choice: University of Chicago’s Islet Transplantation Research Fund, supporting research to advance treatments and improve quality of life for people living with Type 1 diabetes.
WATCH EMMY’S STORY
THE WAY FORWARD 
The mountains don’t erase life’s hardest moments. But they can remind us that dark times won’t last forever. And even when the way ahead feels uncertain, there’s still a world of joy to experience.
