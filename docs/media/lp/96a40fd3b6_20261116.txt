Why is Jackson Hole “The Big One”?
It’s the Wild West.
It’s the mountain steeped in lore.
It’s the deeply personal feeling we all share.
Read the reasons below. Find yours at 10,450 feet.
Before we ever opened, this mountain earned a nickname.
Nothing about the plan was modest: only the second skier tram in North America, with a rise nearly double that of the first. 4,139 continuous vertical feet. Unmatched then. Unmatched now.
In October 1965, two months before the lifts first turned, Skiing magazine said Jackson Hole had to be skied to be believed. The attention drew adventure seekers west, and it didn’t take long for them to give the mountain a name that fit:
The Big One.
By the 70s, “Ski The Big One” had become more than a description. It was part of our identity, appearing alongside the Vertical Foot Club, where skiers earned pins and patches as they stacked vert. But the moniker is more than a measurement. It’s deep snow, wild terrain, iconic views, and the kind of days you remember for the rest of your life.
Decades later, the name has endured because the feeling has. Keep exploring and you’ll find plenty of reasons why:
We’ve always been The Big One.

A couloir with a household name, two national parks as neighbors, snow that's better measured in feet than inches, those famous Tram doors that open into another universe.
There are countless reasons people call us The Big One. But anyone who’s been here knows it's about the one feeling it all adds up to.
It's being somewhere truly wild. Somewhere that makes you feel impossibly small. A shift in your perspective, a new relationship with scale. A place where the spirit of the West is still very much alive, in the people, the culture, and the landscape. A landscape vast enough to quiet everything else. Stillness. But then the rush. You’re utterly alive. It’s the anticipation when the snow simply won't stop falling. Pure adrenaline. Skiing and riding in mountains untamed.
You'll know it when you feel it.
