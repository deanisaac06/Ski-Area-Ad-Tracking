How this was built

Base revenue and paid media

Estimates, not reported figures. Revenue is estimated skier visits times revenue per visit for the resort's tier (destination $120–200, hybrid $90–150, regional $70–120, local $45–80). Base paid media is revenue times 1.2–2.0% for destinations, 0.8–1.5% for hybrids, and 0.3–0.8% for regional and local areas. The anchors are Vail's FY2025 ad expense ($48.0M, about 1.6% of revenue), Bogus Basin's FY2024 990, and the Booyah and Green Glider reports in your project. Canadian figures are rough USD equivalents.

Activity adjustment (new)

Size alone overstates quiet resorts, so base media is multiplied by what each resort is visibly running. Meta active ads: none ×0.6, 1–3 ×0.85, 4–10 ×1.0, 11–20 ×1.15, 20+ ×1.3. Google ads on record: none ×0.6, under 20 ×0.85, 20–99 ×1.0, 100+ ×1.15. A Google video library of 20+ adds ×1.1. The result is held between 0.3 and 1.5, and unchecked signals count as neutral. Mt. Baker, for example, has no Meta or Google ads, so it drops to ×0.36 (about $32K a year).

Why timing matters

The snapshot was taken Nov 7–8, which is pass-deadline season, and about 90% of active ads launched in September–November. A resort that's dark now is very likely a light spender overall. The exceptions are resorts that save budget for mid-season lift tickets and lodging (Dec–Feb), or that run on search alone. That's why zero-activity resorts keep a 0.3 floor rather than going to zero. Re-checking in mid-January would confirm which resorts fall into that group.

Agency economics

Agency revenue = a media management fee (12% of adjusted media) plus creative and production (20% of media, minimum $6K, maximum $150K a year). Gross profit assumes a 60% margin on fees and 35% on production. Expected profit multiplies that by a win factor: A (open) 1.0, small budgets 0.9, an individual/freelancer incumbent 0.65, a group-level decision 0.6, an incumbent agency 0.5, and public or nonprofit owners ×0.8 for procurement. A size-fit factor then reflects that you're a new agency: ×1.0 up to $300K in media, ×0.85 to $800K, ×0.65 to $1.5M, and ×0.45 above that, since the biggest accounts rarely hand their whole budget to a startup. Vail and Alterra resorts score zero.

Ads data

Meta: every resort's page was checked in Meta's Ad Library (US, or Canada for Canadian resorts) on Nov 7–8, 2026. The script recorded each active ad's start date, placements (from the platform icons), asset dimensions and whether it had multiple versions, and saved up to 5 videos per resort (preview quality). Google: the domain's ad count and advertiser of record in the Ads Transparency Center, plus the video-only count for about 50 prospects. For the East and Canada, Google was checked for the open prospects only.

What a "creative mix" score is (and isn't)

The 0–5 score counts toolkit breadth: one point each for using video, building vertical-first, running 3+ formats, using multi-version/dynamic creative, and launching fresh creative since September. It measures how complete a resort's ad toolkit is, not how good the work looks. For visual quality, see the screen grabs and videos and the notes in the Industry read.

Known gaps

No Meta page was found for some resorts (Sugar Mountain, Plattekill, Granite Peak, Whitewater, Sommets, Orford, Pleasant Mountain, Mt. Abram, and Hoodoo, Silver Mountain and Bear Valley in the West). Ownership marked "(verify)" wasn't confirmed. Visit counts are order-of-magnitude.
